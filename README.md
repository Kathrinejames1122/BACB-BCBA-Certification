A dedicated study guide and technical reference repository for candidates preparing for the **Behavior Analyst Certification Board (BACB) Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA) Certification Exam**, covering applied behavior analysis, ethical practice, assessment, intervention, and supervision.

---

## Exam Overview

The BCBA certification validates graduate-level knowledge and skills in behavior analysis, including the assessment of behavior, development of evidence-based interventions, ethical professional practice, and supervision.

- **Exam Format**: Computer-based, multiple-choice examination administered in person at an authorized Pearson VUE testing site.
- **Target Audience**: Behavior analysts and professionals pursuing advanced practice in applied behavior analysis, including clinical, educational, and organizational settings.
- **Prerequisites**: Meet an applicable BACB eligibility pathway, which generally includes a qualifying graduate degree, required behavior-analytic education or experience, and supervised fieldwork. Eligibility must be approved by the BACB before scheduling the examination.

## Core Exam Domains & Knowledge Areas

### 1. Behaviorism and Philosophical Foundations

- Understand the goals of behavior analysis as a science: description, prediction, and control.
- Explain philosophical assumptions such as determinism, empiricism, selectionism, and pragmatism.
- Distinguish behaviorism, experimental analysis, applied behavior analysis, and professional practice.

### 2. Concepts and Principles

- Understand respondent and operant conditioning, reinforcement, and punishment.
- Apply stimulus control, discrimination, generalization, and motivating operations.
- Explain schedules of reinforcement, verbal behavior, extinction, and observational learning.

### 3. Measurement, Data Display, and Interpretation

- Define and measure behavior using direct, indirect, and product measures.
- Apply continuous and discontinuous measurement procedures.
- Graph and interpret behavioral data while evaluating measurement validity and reliability.

### 4. Experimental Design

- Distinguish independent and dependent variables and understand internal and external validity.
- Identify and evaluate reversal, multiple-baseline, multielement, and changing-criterion designs.
- Interpret single-case experimental data and apply experimental design principles.

### 5. Ethical and Professional Issues

- Apply BACB ethics requirements and core principles of professional conduct.
- Protect confidentiality, maintain professional competence, and manage professional relationships.
- Practice cultural humility and follow applicable legal, regulatory, and professional requirements.

### 6. Behavior Assessment

- Review relevant records and incorporate cultural and contextual variables into assessment.
- Design and evaluate preference, descriptive, and functional assessments.
- Interpret assessment findings to identify needs and prioritize meaningful goals.

### 7. Behavior-Change Procedures

- Design and evaluate reinforcement, differential reinforcement, and punishment procedures.
- Apply prompting, shaping, chaining, modeling, and stimulus-control procedures.
- Promote generalization, maintenance, and lasting behavior change.

### 8. Selecting and Implementing Interventions

- Develop observable and measurable intervention goals.
- Select evidence-based interventions aligned with assessment results, client preferences, and context.
- Monitor procedural integrity, evaluate outcomes, and make data-based adjustments.

### 9. Personnel Supervision and Management

- Establish effective supervisory relationships and clear performance expectations.
- Apply evidence-based, culturally responsive supervision and performance-management strategies.
- Evaluate supervisory effectiveness using data and supervisee performance.
